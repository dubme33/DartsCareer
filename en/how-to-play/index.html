How to play DartsCareer

Build a darts career in your browser: choose your player, work towards the next tournament and see how your career develops over the seasons.

This guide is available without starting a career. If you opened it from a game in progress, your game stays in its original tab: return to that tab to continue.

1. Getting started

DartsCareer is a free, single-player darts career simulator. You play against AI opponents, enter tournaments, develop your player and manage a career that can continue across many seasons. There is no required account or app installation to begin in your browser.

  1. Open the game and choose your interface language: English, Polish, German or Dutch.
  2. Choose an existing player or complete the form to create your own. Select a difficulty and how your player supports their career.
  3. In the career hub, check the calendar, your energy and your mailbox before advancing time.

You do not need to use every system immediately. For a first session, check your next eligible event, try a match and learn how to make a save-file backup.

2. Choosing your player

Create a player: enter a first and last name, age and nationality. You can choose gender (male is the default), potential, three different favourite doubles, a dart entry angle and whether to begin with a PDC Tour Card. A nickname, photo and walk-on recording are optional. Potential affects the starting profile and development; OVR is the overall skill rating.

Use the database: select a player and check the displayed OVR and main Order of Merit prize money before starting. This becomes your player rather than a second copy remaining among your AI opponents. Names and media can differ when a community mod is installed.

The Tour Card choice changes your starting position. Starting without a card gives you a route through the qualifying system; starting with a card grants the configured Top 64 start. Existing-player careers use the selected player's profile and card status. Check the preview rather than assuming every player has access to the same events.

3. The hub and career days

The hub shows the career date, player condition, budget and shortcuts to the main systems. Use the calendar for event dates and results, and the mailbox for invitations, payments and career messages. The in-game Tutorial offers further explanations and shortcuts once your career has started.

Simulating a day advances the calendar and processes events in the career world. Training also uses a day, so plan around tournaments. When your event is ready, use its tournament tile to enter; during multi-day events, a later round may be scheduled for another date. Check the next round's date and results before advancing again.

A useful first routine: read the mailbox, check the next tournament, train while you have energy, and leave recovery time before a match. Download a JSON backup after meaningful progress.

4. Playing and simulating matches

In a match, choose a target number and a multiplier — Single, Double or Treble — or aim at a bull target. Use the throw control to throw a dart. Skill, condition and match circumstances affect the result; choosing T20 does not guarantee a treble. The scoring player and the opponent take turns until a leg, set or match is decided.

Standard double-out: reduce your score to exactly zero with a double, including the inner bull. Going below zero, leaving one or reaching zero on an invalid finishing dart is a bust: the visit's scoring is cancelled. A normal leg does not require a double to start scoring.

Other event formats: double-in/double-out requires an opening double in each leg before scoring starts. Master Out allows a finishing double or treble. A tournament can use legs, sets, or a different format in each round. Check the event's rules: these optional settings do not apply to every match.

You can simulate visits, legs or matches using the available controls. Watching your own match lets your player play automatically; pause it or return to manual play when needed. Highlights and eligible AI-versus-AI matches provide other ways to follow the action. Tournament simulation processes the remaining matches and results rather than requiring you to play each opponent's game.

Choose 2D, 3D or TV presentation where available. TV adds a broadcast-style scoreboard and statistics. Set averages are relevant to set matches, and opening-double percentages to double-in matches. 3D availability and smoothness depend on your device and graphics support; 2D is an alternative.

5. Training, energy and form

Scoring and doubles are core skills. Training uses a day and energy; the current system permits two sessions per week. Starting with less effective energy reduces training gains. Check the training screen for the session's cost, remaining weekly sessions and development progress.

Stamina, consistency, mentality and preparation also matter. Staff, equipment and your training base can support training, recovery or preparation. Base OVR and current match performance are different: form, fatigue, momentum and preparation can affect a match without permanently changing the base rating.

Balance practice with recovery rather than training immediately before every event. Career age and development affect the longer-term path; potential is not a promise of a particular tournament result.

6. Tournaments, qualification and rankings

The calendar is your starting point for dates, entry rules, results and prize money. Order of Merit (OOM) means a ranking based on the relevant competition's ranking money. The main OOM uses a rolling two-year period; ProTour, individual tours and custom cycles have their own rules. Your spendable budget and ranking money are separate.

  • PDC Tour Cards: new cards have a two-season guarantee; annual retention and later card allocation also depend on the relevant ranking and card rules. Challenge and Development Tour results provide card routes.
  • Pro Card Trials: this is the base game's Q-School-style competition. UK and EU events each have three First Stage days and four Final Stage days, with daily qualifiers, stage rankings and exemptions. Available cards are split before Final Stage. Attendance and eligibility matter; absence can remove a player's right to a ranking place.
  • Challenge and Development pathways: these are separate tours, not additional money in every ranking. Age, card status and other event conditions determine eligibility. The base game uses fictional tour names in parts of the interface.
  • Women's events: Women's Pro Circuit has 24 scheduled events and a separate seasonal ranking. Women's Global Matchplay is a separate eight-player event. Entry still depends on its qualification rules; gender alone does not guarantee a place.
  • Youth championship: Future Stars Championship has a 128-player field and age-based qualification. Its final takes place during the Players Championship Finals event.

Youth championship and women's matchplay prizes increase career earnings but do not add ranking money to an OOM. Their title and qualification rewards still apply, including the relevant Grand Slam and senior world championship routes. Other majors also use their own ranking cutoffs, qualifiers or previous results; check Qualification & OOM and the event view.

Community mods can display real competition names, including Q-School, Women's Series, Women's World Matchplay and World Youth Championship. This guide describes the game's 1.5.0 systems, not a live real-world ranking or an official sporting rulebook. Custom calendars and qualification rules can change your career's schedule and entry routes.

7. Finances, sponsors and player decisions

Prize money and sponsor income fund your in-game career. Travel, staff salaries, base upkeep and other expenses affect your budget. Ranking prize money is used for standings; spending your budget does not buy a better ranking. These are simulated finances, not real payments to the player.

Regular sponsors can provide income and goals; a technical partner may require its equipment. Staff and infrastructure can support development and preparation. Equipment upgrades and wear are separate from cosmetic dart choices. Leave enough money for recurring costs before a large purchase.

You can choose to focus on darts or combine darts with work. Work brings income with the displayed condition and professionalism tradeoffs. Interviews, press conferences and career events can affect your player's reputation or circumstances. Read the actual choices instead of assuming every answer has the same outcome.

8. A career world that changes

AI players develop, age and can retire. New generations enter the player pool, so future seasons are not just repeats of the starting database. Rivals can improve, lose form or return to stronger performances.

Darts World News, rival records, the Chronicle, Trophy Room and Season Archive help you follow this world. News stories are generated from the simulated events of your career. They are not reports of current real-world darts results, even when a mod supplies real names or photographs.

9. Editors and customisation

Use the career's editors to edit players, add players, manage national teams or build a calendar. Optional hometowns and preferred scoring targets let you adjust player profiles. Dart personalisation includes component colours, conventional shaft lengths and the angle at which darts sit in the board.

The tournament editor supports event duplication, entry conditions, qualification routes from a custom tour ranking, round-specific legs or sets, groups and knockout play. Optional double-in and Master Out settings belong to the event's rules. Tour rankings, cards, age, gender, nationality and city restrictions can determine who is eligible.

Changes apply to your career and supported exported packs; editing does not alter the game for other users. Download a backup before clearing rankings, removing cards or rebuilding the calendar. A qualifying cycle needs a ranking and events that finish before the target tournament.

10. Installing a community mod

  1. Download a mod ZIP intended for your version of DartsCareer. If you already have a career, export a JSON backup first.
  2. Use Upload Mod (.ZIP) on the start screen or the mod upload in the career's More section.
  3. Select the original ZIP. A supported package contains one mod.json manifest, at its root or inside a folder, and any media it references. You do not import a save JSON through this control.
  4. Wait for the import to finish and read any message. If the mod changes the starting database, loading it before a new career gives you those choices at player selection.

Supported mods can supply names, player photos, tournament data, audio and board or surround artwork. Media such as additional caller voices depend on the mod's files. Mods are community content; use the author's installation instructions and respect the rights to included material.

The browser can remember an imported mod, but storage availability and limits matter. Keep the original mod ZIP as well as your career backup. A career JSON is not a replacement for every separately cached mod file; install the compatible mod on another device when needed.

11. Saving, backing up and moving a career

Save Game stores the current career locally in your browser. Load Game restores the browser save. Storage uses IndexedDB with legacy or fallback localStorage support; it is tied to the browser and site, not an online account. A different browser or device does not automatically receive that career.

Keep JSON backups. Clearing site data, private browsing, browser cleanup, storage limits or changing devices can leave you without a local save. Download a file regularly, especially before a major edit, a game update or a long break. Keep more than one dated copy.

Download a backup

  1. Return to the career hub and open More.
  2. Select Download Save File. Your browser downloads a darts_career_save_….json file.
  3. Find it in your device's downloads and copy it somewhere you control. Wait for the download to finish before leaving.

Restore or transfer it

  1. Copy the JSON file to the other device. Keep the original file as a backup.
  2. Open DartsCareer and choose Upload save file (.JSON) on the start screen, or the save-file upload under More.
  3. Select the JSON, wait for confirmation and check the player and career date. Importing replaces the currently open career, so back that up first if you need it.
  4. Install the compatible community mod separately if its assets are needed, then make a fresh backup after playing.

There is no automatic cloud synchronisation. Do not treat a bookmark, an installed website shortcut or a mod ZIP as a career backup.

12. Frequently asked questions

Is DartsCareer free?

Yes. You can start and play a career for free. The budget, prizes and sponsorship money described here are in-game values.

Do I need to install an app?

No. Open the game in a browser. An optional home-screen shortcut is not required and does not provide a separate cloud save.

Do I need an account?

No account or login is required to create and play a local career.

Can I play on a phone?

The interface supports mobile layouts. Graphics support and performance vary; use 2D if 3D is unavailable or runs poorly. Back up the save file on the phone too.

Where is my save stored?

In the local site storage of the browser you used. It is not automatically shared with another browser, device or account. Clearing that storage can remove it.

How do I download a JSON save?

In the career hub, open More and choose Download Save File. Keep the downloaded JSON; clicking Save Game alone creates only a browser save. See the backup steps.

How do I move my career to another device?

Transfer the exported JSON, then use Upload save file (.JSON) in the other device's browser. Keep and install the compatible mod ZIP separately if required.

How do I install a mod?

Use Upload Mod (.ZIP) and select a compatible original mod package containing mod.json. Wait for completion. See the installation steps.

Is there online multiplayer?

No. The current game provides a single-player career with AI opponents and options to watch matches; these are not online matches between users.

Can I rely on playing entirely offline?

No full offline mode is provided. Some game resources need a network connection. Installing a website shortcut does not guarantee offline availability.

Where can I report a problem?

Use Contact. Include the browser, device, what happened and the steps leading to it. Keep a JSON backup when investigating a career problem, and avoid sharing unnecessary personal media or details.
